# Lightweight UX Brief — in plain words

> How the place is organized, how people walk through it, how we know it works, who we include, how we speak. Written in English. Every test name, button, and place keeps its Romanian wording where a real person would see it.

---

## 1. How the site is organized — the map of the place

Think of the site as a small, calm building with clear signs. No maze, few doors at the top, everything else reached by searching or by following a concern.

**The shape, top to bottom:**

* **Home** — a quiet welcome that says what we do in one line, a big search that understands Romanian with and without diacritics, a small row of concerns in plain words, a short shelf of common tests, a short shelf of bundles, and a calm list of labs.
* **Search and results** — one place where you type and see results as you type, with gentle hints. Results show one clear card for each test, the same name everywhere.
* **A single test page** — for example *TSH* or *Vitamina D (25-OH)*. It shows what the test watches in one sentence, the price side by side across labs, and under that the small honest line for the collection fee. It also says what sample is needed (*Sânge, Urină, Frotiu*) and any simple note like fasting.
* **A bundle page** — for example *Profil tiroidian* or *Lipidogramă*. It says in plain words what is inside — *TSH, T3 liber, T4 liber, Anti-TPO* — and then shows, for each lab that sells a ready bundle, how many of those parts are inside and what is missing. A short note above the table explains what the bundle watches, who it is often for, and what to read next. This is the quiet companion we add if you welcome the steady watcher — plain explanation above price, not instead of it.
* **Labs and their branches** — a page for each lab like *Synevo, Invitro, Sante, MedExpert, Alfa*, and a map that shows branches with hours, phone, and sample type, filterable by lab and by sample.
* **Your lists** — a place where you keep named lists like *Analizele mele* or *Mama — control anual*. You can add from a search or from inside a bundle, make a new list, and send a link that opens for someone else with no account.
* **Comparison** — a table for up to a handful of tests where you see, in one glance, the honest cheapest place if you go to one lab, and what you would save if you split — without feeling pushed to split.
* **A quiet coming soon** — where booking will later live. For now a calm card: *Soon you will book here. For now, write to hello@laborata.md.*

**Languages and addresses:** the same test lives at one address, written from its Romanian name — *tsh, hemoglobina, glucoza, profil-tiroidian* — with a second skeleton for English (*tests* instead of *analize*, *labs* instead of *laboratoare*) that points to the same thing. Old addresses redirect to the new one if we ever change a name. No one should lose a link.

**What the building does not have:** no accounts to create, no heavy filters wall, no yearly membership pitch inside the comparison.

## 2. How people walk through it — three walks, end to end

### Walk one — hunting for a fair price for a few tests

*You are the person with a paper. You have *TSH, Hemoleucogramă, Feritină*.*

You land on the home. You see a calm line — *Find the right tests, at a fair price* — and a big search. You type *tsh*. Even without the little marks, it finds *TSH*. You tap the card. The test page shows what TSH watches in one sentence, then a small table: *Sante 110 lei, Synevo 120 lei, Invitro 135 lei — plus 30 lei for blood draw, once*. You tap *Save*. You make a list called *Analizele mele*. You go back, search *feritină*, save it to the same list. At the top you see *2 tests in your list* and a link to *Compare*. You open comparison and see, in one glance: *Cheapest in one place — Sante, 260 lei total* and, smaller, *If you split, you could save 18 lei, but you would go twice*. You choose one place, open the map, filter to *Sante* and *Sânge*, see a branch in *Botanica* open Saturday with a phone number, and you share the list to your family as a link.

*Alternate path:* you mistype *feritina* without the mark, it still finds *Feritină*. You add a test that is not sold at one lab — that lab simply says *Not available* in gray, not hidden.

### Walk two — understanding what a bundle really is

*You are the doctor or the careful shopper looking at *Profil tiroidian*.*

You open *Profil tiroidian* from a search or from a concern row like *Hormones*. At the top a short explainer says in plain words what it watches — thyroid health — who it is often for, and that it usually includes *TSH, T3 liber, T4 liber, Anti-TPO*. Under that, a table shows each lab that sells a ready bundle: *Sante — 4 of 4 parts, Synevo — 4 of 4, Invitro — 3 of 4 (missing Anti-TPO)*. If you want all four, you can add all parts at once or one by one. If a bundle is sold but not as a ready pack everywhere, the page says so plainly — *Not yet sold as a bundle here* — and does not invent it.

*Alternate path:* you open a bundle with many tests like *Lipidogramă* and see quickly which bundle covers it fully and which leaves one marker out, so you can decide bundle versus buying one by one.

### Walk three — finding a nearby place that is open

*You are the family list keeper on Saturday morning.*

You open *Labs → See branches* or tap *View on map* from a list. The map shows branches you can filter by lab and by sample type — *Sânge, Urină, Frotiu* — and a small field to search a street or neighbourhood. You see the count — *12 branches match* — and a side list with name, address, hours, phone. You tap one; the map centers, the card says *Open now* or *Opens at 8:00*, and you copy the address.

*Alternate path:* you filtered to *MedExpert only* and there are no matching branches nearby — the page says so kindly and suggests widening the filter or seeing all branches, not showing an empty map.

All three walks end the same way if you want: you share the list as a link that opens for someone else with no account, showing the same prices and the same branch.

## 3. How we know it works — plain measures

We do not hide behind percentages. We watch a few human things:

* **Can someone answer *where should I go and how much will I pay, fees included* quickly?** Under half a minute from home to comparison for three tests, on a phone, without opening another tab.
* **Do people keep and share lists?** Lists made, lists with two or more tests, shared links opened on another phone with no account — and that the shared page reads correctly.
* **Is the bundle information true, not guessed?** No bundle shown as covering a test it does not; no bundle invented where a lab does not sell one. The gaps are shown honestly as *Not yet sold here*.
* **Is the site quick and readable?** On a small phone at the common size, and on a large screen; in light and in dark; with a clean reading score and a clean help-for-everyone score when we run the automatic checker before each publish.
* **Do people return because the words were plain?** A second visit to the same bundle or list feels calm because the short explainer above the table made sense the first time.

If any of these slip, we fix that walk before adding anything new.

## 4. Who we include — help for everyone, not an extra

The site must feel easy for everyone, not as an extra afterthought:

* **Contrast you can read.** Body text is easy on the eyes against its background, large text a little easier. No pale gray on white.
* **You can use it without a mouse.** Everything you can tap you can also reach with a keyboard, with a visible ring where you are, and you can close any open panel with Escape.
* **You can hear it.** Headings are in order, buttons say what they do, lists are lists, and no picture is left without a short description.
* **Motion respects you.** If you asked your device to reduce motion, we keep things still — no bouncy hero, no slipping panels.
* **Language is correct.** The page says *lang="ro"* when you are in Romanian, *lang="en"* in English, so a reader can pronounce *Sânge* correctly.

We build on parts that already care about this, and we check every new piece with a keyboard and a small screen before we say it is done. Every screen we later draw in the playground will be checked at the small phone size and on a large screen, in light and in dark, with no data, one item, and many.

## 5. How we speak — honest, calm, precise

Like a clean, well-printed form you trust. Direct, not chatty. Confident, not loud. The words earn trust through clarity, not through warmth layered on top.

* **Plain words first, codes second.** We say *Energy, mood & sleep* before *TSH*. We say *Lipidogramă* and then, in smaller type, list *Colesterol total, HDL, LDL, Trigliceride*.
* **Prices plainly, everywhere the same.** We write *270 lei* in Romanian and *270 MDL* in English, grouped, rounded to whole lei, with the small collection fee shown once — *plus 30 lei for blood draw, once* or *0 lei — included* — never hidden until the last step.
* **Bundles explained, not just listed.** Above each bundle table, a short sentence says what the bundle watches and who it is often for, before the numbers. This is the quiet companion for the steady watcher — plain explanation above price.
* **Where the truth came from, plainly said.** Each price carries a small note of where it was last seen and when, and every bundle that is not sold somewhere says so plainly — *Not yet sold as a bundle here* — so honest gaps are never mistaken for low prices.
* **No dark patterns, no hidden push.** The cheapest honest place is shown plainly; splitting is shown as a small *you could save this much if you go twice* without pushing you to go twice. No sponsored ordering inside the table, no star ratings that pretend to judge labs.

**The feeling to keep:** as if you handed that small paper to someone calm who laid the prices side by side on a clean table, with every small fee visible, and said *here — go here, pay this, bring this sample, they are open then*.

---

## 6. What we leave out, for now

Booking a visit inside the site — that needs real agreements with labs and a careful next step. It stays as a quiet *Coming soon* card with a reachable address, not a form that pretends to book.

---

This brief is the one paper we point to when we choose what to draw and what to leave out. If a screen does not help one of the people above do one of the walks above, with this voice and this care, we do not build it now.
