# Who we help — three people, in plain words

> Written in English. Every test name, button, and place keeps its Romanian wording where a real person would see it on screen. No code, no diagrams — just stories we can point to when we choose what to build.

---

## 1. The person with a paper in hand

**Who they are**

A woman in her early thirties in Chișinău. Her family doctor just handed her a small paper: *TSH, Hemoleucogramă completă, Feritină, Vitamina D*. She is on her phone on the bus. She does not care about lab brands. She cares about paying a fair price, not going to two places, and knowing if she needs to come on an empty stomach.

**What they are trying to get done**

> *When I have three or four tests written down, I want to see the honest cheapest place, with every small fee shown, so I can go once and not overpay.*

**What hurts today**

She opens three lab sites in three tabs. Each one writes the same test a little differently — *Vitamina D (25-OH)* in one place, *25-OH Vitamina D totală* in another — and the small collection fee (*Prelevarea sângelui venos*) is sometimes shown, sometimes hidden until the end. She is not sure if *Profil tiroidian* in one lab is the same as in another. She copies prices by hand.

**What good looks like for her**

She types *tsh* or *vitamina d* and the site understands, even without the little marks above letters. She sees one clear card for each test, with the same name everywhere. Under it she sees prices side by side — *Synevo 120 lei, Invitro 135 lei, Sante 110 lei* — and under that, the small honest line: *plus 30 lei for blood draw, once*. She can add the three tests to a list named *Analizele mele* with one tap, see the honest cheapest place in one glance, and keep the address.

**How we know we helped her**

She can answer *where should I go and how much will I pay, fees included* in under half a minute, without opening another tab.

## 2. The person looking after someone else

**Who they are**

A man in his early forties. His mother needs tests, his child needs tests next week. He is not a medical person. He wants to keep things tidy and to send a link to his wife, not a screenshot.

**What they are trying to get done**

> *When I order for family, I want to keep a short named list, see where the branches are on a map with hours, and send that list as a simple link to someone at home.*

**What hurts today**

His phone is full of photos of papers. He forgets which list was for whom. One lab is closer to work, another is open on Saturday. He does not want to make an account just to save a list.

**What good looks like for him**

He makes a list called *Mama — control anual* and another called *Copil — septembrie*. He adds tests from a search or from a bundle, picks a lab branch near *Botanica* that is open Saturday, and taps *Share*. The link opens for his wife without her needing to sign in. The link shows the same prices and the same branch, written plainly.

**How we know we helped him**

He can make a second list without losing the first, and a shared link opens and reads correctly on another phone with no account.

## 3. The doctor who recommends

**Who they are**

A family doctor who sees ten patients before lunch. She writes the same handful of tests often — *TSH, T3 liber, T4 liber, Anti-TPO* for a thyroid check; *Hemoleucogramă, Feritină, Vitamina B12, Folat* for anemia. She does not sell labs. She wants her patients to actually go and not be confused.

**What they are trying to get done**

> *When I recommend tests, I want to hand over a clear, shareable list with plain names, where a bundle like *Profil tiroidian* is explained as plain parts, so my patient can act at once and not call back with questions.*

**What hurts today**

She writes names by hand, shortens them, patients mistype them later. A patient hears *Profil anemie* and thinks it is one test, not five. A patient thinks *Profil tiroidian* is the same everywhere, but each lab puts slightly different tests inside.

**What good looks like for her**

She can, in a minute, open *Profil tiroidian*, see exactly which tests are inside — *TSH, T3 liber, T4 liber, Anti-TPO* — see which vendor bundles cover them well and which miss one, add the parts she wants to a list, and give the patient a single link. The link is calm, plain, and honest about what is inside the bundle versus what must be bought one by one.

**How we know we helped her**

A patient can open the link, read the same words the doctor used, and go to the right branch without calling back.

## 4. The short list of jobs, in one place

These are the jobs we design for. If a job is not here, it does not need a screen in the first version.

1. **Find a test I already have in mind.** I type *tsh* or *glicemie* and I find one clear card with the same name everywhere, even if I forget the little marks.

2. **See the honest price, fees included.** I see prices side by side, with the small collection fee (*Prelevarea sângelui venos — 30 lei*, or *0 lei* where it is included) shown once, not hidden.

3. **Understand what a bundle really is.** I open *Profil tiroidian* or *Lipidogramă* and see plainly which tests are inside, which labs sell it as a ready bundle, and what is missing if I buy that bundle.

4. **Keep a short list and share it.** I make a named list like *Analizele mele* or *Mama — control*, add tests from search or from inside a bundle, and send it as a link that opens for someone else with no account.

5. **Compare up to a handful of tests at once.** I add three to five tests and see, in one glance, the cheapest honest place if I go to one lab, and what I would save if I split — without feeling pushed to split.

6. **Find a nearby place that is open.** I see branches on a map, filter by lab and by what sample they can take (*Sânge, Urină, Frotiu*), and see hours and phone plainly.

7. **Feel safe that the words are true.** I see where the price came from and when it was last seen, in plain language, and I am not pushed toward one lab because it paid.
