# What the good sites teach us — three reads, in plain words

> How we looked: each site opened in its own browser session, in parallel. We read the home, the shop of tests, the way concerns are grouped, the short quiz that builds a panel, and the membership and help pages. We did not copy pixels — we noted patterns, people, and jobs, in plain words. No code, just what a visitor can see and feel.

We kept MitoHealth as the anchor you named, and added Function Health and Superpower — two modern health memberships that, like us, sell the feeling of *you finally understand your labs*, but from a different angle.

---

## 1. MitoHealth — calm shop that speaks in concerns, not codes

**What it feels like**

A quiet, spacious shop. Top has a few words only — *Tests, Shop All, Lab tests, Scans, Membership* — and a second line that says *Browse by concern*: *Energy, mood & sleep; Heart; Hormones; Gut; Nutrient deficiencies; Inflammation; Cancer & early detection*. You do not need a code. You can start with *Which tests for low energy?* or ask the small concierge box in plain words.

Farther down, the home is gentle — *Stay ahead of your health*, *Catch issues early and feel your best longer*, with real voices from Trustpilot and a promise of clear prices and a clinician looking over results. You can buy one small test like *Ferritin — 5.85* or a ready panel like *Mito Core — 197* for a yearly picture.

The shop page itself is a soft catalog: you can filter by concern, by type of test, by lab, sort by price. If you are lost, a card says *Answer a few questions and we will build a panel tailored to you, including what you can skip.*

That card leads to a short quiz — nine steps, each one light: *What matters most right now? How old are you? How have you been feeling? Anything in the family? What do you take already?* At the end it asks for an email to send the plan. The whole tone is guiding, not diagnosing: *thorough health checkup, get to the bottom of symptoms, understand family risks, focus on longevity.*

**Who it seems to love**

A person who pays to be watched with care and wants to understand without arguing with a busy doctor. Someone who says *My mom had cancer young, where do I start?* or *I am tired and want deeper insights than a yearly check*. They want a guide who picks what matters and tells them what they can skip, at a fair, open price.

In jobs, in plain words: *When I feel something is off but I do not know where to start, I want a trusted guide to build a small, honest panel for my goals and risks, so I do not buy too much and I do not miss what matters.*

**What to steal, what to leave**

* **Steal:** speaking in concerns first, codes second; a small plain-words quiz that builds a panel and says what to skip; a calm home that names price and clinician review in one breath; the idea that a panel is explained as *what it is good for* before listing markers.
* **Leave:** the heavier shop filters that feel empty when no test matches; the quiet push toward membership before you have seen a single honest price.
* **Watch:** the concierge search box — asking in words *low energy* is close to how our own search should feel, even though their search box felt empty when we looked.

## 2. Function Health — a steady, yearly check with depth you can count

**What it feels like**

A bolder, more numbers-first story. The home says, in large type: *Check your health. Every year. 160+ lab tests, 1,000+ conditions. 365 per year — 1 per day.* Three steps, very plain: *160+ tests at 2,000 labs or we come to you; results explained by top doctors; follow your plan and test again.* Under that, long quiet ribbons of conditions — *Anemia, Gout, Fatty liver, Pancreatic cancer* — that move slowly, as if to say *we look wide, not thin*.

Tests are grouped by what they watch: *Hormones & thyroid, Cancer & silent risks, Heart & metabolic, Aging, Mental health*. Each group shows counts and a few marker names — *TSH, Testosterone, Galleri, ApoB, Vitamin D, hs-CRP*. Each group says *how many* and *what kind*, before the long list.

A second strong promise appears everywhere: *Establish your baseline, tracked for life. Monitor how your body changes.* You can bring your past labs, share with your doctor, or connect to a chat helper. The guides section is a small library — *Mental Health 101, Brain Health 101, Gut Health 101* — written as short, titled booklets, not long blog posts. Pricing is one number, no maze. Help pages say plainly where testing happens and that it is not for emergencies.

**Who it seems to love**

A person who thinks in years, not visits — who wants one careful check each year, watched over time, explained by doctors they trust. Someone who says *I want a whole-body picture, explained, with a plan I can follow and then re-check*. Often a little more medical in language, comfortable seeing *Lipoprotein (a)* or *ApoB* by name.

In jobs: *When I want to watch health steadily with one honest yearly check, I want a wide, doctor-chosen set explained plainly and kept in one place over time, so I can see change early and share it with my doctor.*

**What to steal, what to leave**

* **Steal:** the yearly baseline story — *establish, track for life, watch change*; the grouping by *what we watch* with a short count before the long list; the plain promise of sharing with your doctor and keeping one longitudinal record; the guide library that turns markers into short, titled stories.
* **Leave:** the heavier numbers-first tone at the very top (160, 1,000) that can feel loud for someone who just has a small paper with three names; the long moving ribbons that are pretty but not scannable.
* **Watch:** the *bring your past labs* idea — close to our own shared list over time, but more heavyweight than we need for day one.

## 3. Superpower — membership that starts with a full-body test and keeps you close

**What it feels like**

The most membership-forward of the three, and the most *we stay with you*. The home says: *Your new health membership — members start with 100+ lab tests. 199 per year. 1M biomarkers tested.* Four steps, very tactile: *Test your baseline — one simple blood draw; Results explained — a complete picture in one safe place; Build your protocol — backed by evidence, shaped by your data; Access everything — act, then retest. You are in control.*

Under that, a clear value mirror: *What could cost 10,000 is now 199* and a list that feels generous — *100+ biomarker test, detect early signs, upload past bloodwork, biological age, personal protocol for diet and lifestyle and supplements, wearable link (Apple Health, Whoop, Oura), AI chat with context on your health, 24/7 access to a care team, add-on tests like gut and toxins and Grail Galleri, peptides, prescriptions*. The tone is confident, with Stanford, Harvard, Oxford marks and a medical board of familiar faces.

Guides are a huge, well-kept library — thousands of short explainers, grouped finely: *Heart & cardiovascular, Hormone testing, Gut health, Heavy metals, Environmental toxins, Longevity, Sleep*. The search inside guides feels like a small search engine for *what could this mean*.

Help pages feel close: *Message your private care team 24/7 — in your pocket at all times.*

**Who it seems to love**

A person who wants *one place and one team that stays with them*, who will pay a small yearly fee to feel held. Someone who says *I want a full-body check to start, a plan shaped by my data, and someone I can message when I worry*. They are comfortable being called a member, not a patient, and they like the feeling of *we run a lot, so you do not have to guess*.

In jobs: *When I want a full, affordable baseline and a team that stays with me, I want 100+ markers in one draw and a personal plan I can act on and re-check, so I feel safe without chasing labs myself.*

**What to steal, what to leave**

* **Steal:** the four-step story with verbs you can picture — *test, see, build, act then retest*; the idea that a member brings past labs and wearables into one place; the very fine guide library that turns a marker like *Ferritin* into a short, humane answer.
* **Leave:** the membership-first framing that can feel pushy for someone who just wants a fair price for three tests this week; the long list of *we also sell peptides and prescriptions* that is far from our honest price comparison job.
* **Watch:** the care-team chat — warm, but we are not a care team; we are a fair comparison that helps you reach the right branch.

## What the three have in common — patterns worth keeping in mind

These are the patterns, not pixels, that kept appearing:

* **Concerns before codes.** All three let you start with *low energy, heart, hormones, gut* before showing *TSH* or *ApoB*. MitoHealth does this most softly; Function and Superpower do it with grouped counts and short marker hints.
* **Panels explained as jobs, not lists.** Each ready panel says *good for yearly baseline* or *good for deeper picture* before listing 117 or 138 markers. The number comes after the meaning.
* **Baseline over time, in one place.** Function and Superpower both promise *one record, tracked for life, share with your doctor or AI, bring past labs*. That feeling of *one place* keeps coming back.
* **Guides as a living shelf.** All three keep a guide or insight library that turns a scary marker into a short, calm explainer. MitoHealth leans to cost explainers (*thyroid panel cost*), Function to short 101s, Superpower to a huge, finely grouped shelf.
* **Membership with a plain price.** Each says the yearly price early and plainly — *at our cost*, *1 per day*, *what could cost 10,000 is now 199* — and then lists what is included.

These patterns feel close to our own needs — clear panel meaning, plain sharing, one honest price story — even though our job is price comparison across five labs, not selling a membership.

---

## What they do not do — and why it matters for us

None of the three helps you answer *which lab near me is cheapest for the exact three tests I have on paper, fees included, and where can I go that is open today*. Their price promise is membership-wide (*199 or 365 covers a bundle*), not *Synevo 120, Sante 110, plus 30 for blood draw, at Botanica on Saturday*. Their map, when it exists, is a network map, not a branch-level *open now, phone here, sample type here* map.

That gap is ours to keep and to fill well.

---

## Small notes on how they feel to use

* **Top navigation is light.** Three to five words at the top, never a wall of links. Filters, when they appear, are soft chips, not heavy dropdowns.
* **Empty and "not sure" states are kind.** MitoHealth says *Not sure where to start? Answer a few questions and we will build a panel, including what you can skip.* Function says *Start testing / See how it works* side by side, so hesitation has a gentle second door. Superpower says *All your health data, in one place* when there is not yet data.
* **Help is always one tap away.** Membership pages repeat *message anytime, clinician reviewed, HSA eligible* in the same breath as the price — so the price never feels naked.

We will keep these feelings in mind when we draw our own empty, loading, and error states.
