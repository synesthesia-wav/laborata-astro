# Who the good sites seem to serve — and what it means for us

> In plain words. Each site implies a person by how it speaks, what it offers, and what it promises. We name those implied people, phrase their jobs, lay them beside our three, and decide — with your yes — whether to welcome anyone new.

---

## Who the good sites seem to love

### From MitoHealth — the guided shopper who wants to be picked for

**In one sentence:** Someone who feels unsure where to start and wants a trusted guide to pick a small, honest panel for their goals and risks, and to tell them what they can skip.

**Clues we saw:** *Build my panel* as a first-class entry; a nine-step quiz that asks about what matters now, age, how you feel, family history, and what you take; concerns like *Energy, mood & sleep* before marker codes; panels described as *Your yearly baseline* before listing 117 markers; the home asking *My mom had cancer young, where do I start?*

**Jobs, in plain words:**

* *When I do not know where to start, I want a guide to build a small panel for my goals and risks, so I do not buy too much and I do not miss what matters.*
* *When I worry about a family risk, I want to see concerns like *heart* or *thyroid* in plain words before I see codes, so I feel safe choosing.*

**What they are hired to do:** reassure, narrow choice, pick for you.

### From Function Health — the steady tracker who thinks in years

**In one sentence:** Someone who wants one careful, wide check each year, explained by doctors, kept in one place over time so change is visible early.

**Clues we saw:** *Check your health. Every year. 160+ tests, 1,000 conditions. 365 per year.* *Establish your baseline, tracked for life. Monitor how your body changes.* Long, slow ribbons of conditions; guides as short 101s; a strong *bring past labs, share with your doctor or AI* promise; a single yearly price, plainly said.

**Jobs:**

* *When I want to watch health steadily with one yearly check, I want a wide, doctor-chosen set explained plainly and kept in one place, so I can see change early.*
* *When I want to learn, I want a short guide like *Gut Health 101* that turns a scary marker into a calm story, so I know what to do next.*

**What they are hired to do:** watch, explain, keep history.

### From Superpower — the member who wants one place and a close team

**In one sentence:** Someone who wants a full-body baseline and then a team that stays close — a plan shaped by data, past labs and wearables in one place, and someone to message when they worry.

**Clues we saw:** *Your new health membership — 100+ tests in one draw, 199 per year. 1M biomarkers tested.* Four tactile steps: *Test, see, build protocol, act then retest.* A long, generous membership list — *biological age, personal protocol, wearable link, AI chat with context, 24/7 care team, add-on tests like Grail Galleri*. A huge guide shelf, finely grouped. *Message your private care team 24/7.*

**Jobs:**

* *When I want a full, affordable baseline and a team that stays with me, I want 100+ markers in one draw and a plan I can act on and recheck, so I feel held without chasing labs myself.*
* *When I have a question at night, I want to message a care team that knows my numbers, so I do not sit with worry.*

**What they are hired to do:** hold, personalize, stay close.

## Our three people, again — short

* **Paper in hand** — three or four names from the doctor, on a phone, wants the honest cheapest place, fees shown, one trip.
* **Family list keeper** — orders for two, keeps named lists, checks the map for a branch open Saturday, sends a link without making an account.
* **Doctor who recommends** — wants to hand over a clear, shareable list where a bundle like *Profil tiroidian* is explained as plain parts, so the patient can act at once.

Their jobs, already named in the previous note, are about finding, comparing with fees, understanding bundles, keeping and sharing lists, and reaching a branch that is open.

## Should we welcome someone new?

We said yes to welcoming if the reading suggests someone valuable. The reading suggests one newcomer is valuable and not heavy — and a second, lighter companion.

### Proposed newcomer — the steady watcher

**Who they are, in one sentence:** A person who wants to understand health steadily over time, not just this week's paper — someone who wants plain explanations alongside a price and a way to see change if they keep their lists.

**Why they matter for us:** They are the common thread across all three good sites — *establish a baseline, track for life, see change early, plain guides*. Welcoming them makes our bundle pages more than *cheaper here* — they become *here is what is inside, what each part watches, and what to do next, in plain words*. They also give our lists a gentle reason to return: *you kept this list in spring, here is how it reads now*.

**What they would ask of us, in jobs:**

* *When I keep a list over months, I want plain next to each test what it watches (for example *Feritină — iron stores*) and a small note of when the price was last seen, so I trust the page tomorrow as well as today.*
* *When I am unsure about a bundle, I want a short guide like *What is Lipidogramă* in plain words before the table, so I choose with calm, not fear.*

**What we would build differently if we welcome them:** we would write short, kind explainers for the most common bundles and tests, and we would show lists with a faint sense of time — *saved now, last seen price here* — without turning the site into a membership or a chat.

**What we would not do:** we would not become a care team, we would not sell a yearly coaching plan, we would not push membership language. Their steady watching stays light — plain words next to price, not a dashboard of scores.

**Risk if we add:** a little more writing and care for bundle explainers; a temptation to add coaching tone that would feel heavy for someone in a hurry.

**Risk if we do not add:** bundle pages stay as price tables only, and we miss the chance to be the calm explainer the good sites prove people value.

**Our light suggestion:** welcome them as a quiet fourth, not a loud one. Keep price as the headline, add explanation as the gentle companion.

### Optional companion — the curious learner

A lighter echo of the same person — someone who arrives not with a paper but with a question like *Which tests for low energy?* MitoHealth invites this with *Build my panel* and *concerns*; Function and Superpower do it with guide shelves.Welcoming them costs little: a concern entry on the home — *Energy, sleep, Heart, Hormones* — that leads to the right tests and bundles in plain words. We can treat this as part of the same fourth, not a fifth person, by giving the home a soft concern row without changing the search-first heart of the site.

## What stays, what shifts

* **Stays:** our three people remain the decision makers. Every screen must still serve one of their jobs. Price stays the headline, shown side by side with fees included, neutral across five labs. Lists stay named and shareable with no account. Branches stay on a real map with hours.

* **Shifts, lightly, if you say yes to the fourth:** bundle and test pages gain a short, plain explainer above the table — *what this watches, who it is often for, what to read next* — and lists gain a faint sense of time. The home gains a small concern row that speaks the same words as the quiz-less version of *Build my panel* — no quiz yet, just plain guidance.

* **Does not shift:** we do not add membership, chat, scoring, or yearly coaching. Those belong to the good sites, not to our honest comparison job.
